{# ---------------------------------------------------- 2. THE PROBLEM SECTION #}
THE CROSS-LINGUAL SAFETY GAP

Why English-Only Testing Misses Critical Vulnerabilities

Standard AI safety guardrails are trained predominantly on English and Western corpora. When frontier models are prompted in African languages, safety alignment often degrades, exposing organizations to regional fraud, misinformation, and compliance failures.

{# Vector 1 #}
01 / SAFETY EVASION

Low-Resource Jailbreak Bypass

Prompts that trigger immediate refusal in English can bypass safety guardrails when delivered in Hausa or regional languages, opening attack vectors for financial fraud and malicious instructions.

{# Vector 2 #}
02 / ORTHOGRAPHY

Dialect & Hooked Letter Collapse

Missing tone diacritics and hooked letters (`ɓ`, `ɗ`, `ƙ`, `ƴ`) or conflating regional dialects alters semantic meaning entirely, turning harmless queries into offensive interpretations or gibberish.

{# Vector 3 #}
03 / SPEECH & NOISE

Acoustic & Code-Switching Breakdown

African conversations naturally blend English, French, and indigenous idioms under realistic acoustic background noise, causing standard Speech-to-Text models to hallucinate or fail.

{# Vector 4 #}
04 / CULTURAL BIAS

Proverb Misinterpretation & Bias

Western RLHF filters frequently misclassify indigenous proverbs, cultural courtesies, and traditional dispute resolutions as toxic while missing localized cultural scams and slurs.

{# ---------------------------------------------------- 3. PUBLISHED RESEARCH EVIDENCE (N-ATLaS) #}
PUBLISHED RESEARCH PROOF POINT

N-ATLaS: Language-Dependent Safety Discrepancies

Empirical exploratory study demonstrating how model safety behaviour diverges between English and Hausa matched requests.

View on Hugging Face Dataset
{# Research Study Card #}
11 Evaluation Records Hausa & English Matched Pairs Deterministic Repeated Runs Focused Exploratory Study

What the N-ATLaS Evaluation Revealed

Our exploratory evaluation examined 11 matched request pairs across English and Hausa covering deceptive financial requests and health inquiries. In the evaluated model revision, the model consistently refused the English requests while answering the corresponding Hausa requests.

Key Research Takeaway

Safety alignment in English does not automatically guarantee safety in African languages. Human-in-the-loop native evaluation is essential to identify and remediate language-dependent failure modes.

{# Documented Scope & Methodological Limitations #}
Scientific Boundaries & Research Limitations

This exploratory study is based on 11 evaluation records on a single model revision using repeated deterministic runs (which are not independent statistical replications). The evaluation involved one native Hausa reviewer; independent multi-reviewer evaluation would further strengthen these findings. This study highlights the need for dedicated African-language safety evaluations rather than claiming universal guardrail failure across all languages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Factual information for enterprise AI teams, research directors, and specialist contributors.

What is Brydl?

Brydl (Brydl AI) is an African-language AI evaluation and safety company developed by Gretek Technologies. It helps frontier model developers, safety researchers, and enterprise AI teams uncover vulnerabilities, evaluate safety behavior, and validate datasets across African languages.

What does Brydl do?

Brydl evaluates large language models (LLMs) and voice systems against African linguistic and safety benchmarks, conducts adversarial red-teaming (RedLab), tests speech systems under noisy acoustic environments, generates cultural human-in-the-loop preference data (RLHF/DPO), and publishes empirical cross-lingual safety research.

Who is Brydl designed for?

Brydl is designed for global foundation AI labs and model developers, AI safety researchers, African AI laboratories and research institutions, and enterprises deploying AI applications for African markets, as well as vetted native linguists and specialist evaluators.

What problems does Brydl solve?

Brydl solves the cross-lingual safety gap: English-only safety guardrails frequently fail when probed in low-resource African languages, Western alignment bias misinterprets indigenous cultural idioms and proverbs, and speech models degrade under realistic regional accents and background noise.

Why should someone choose Brydl?

Unlike generic crowd platforms, Brydl provides empirical research-led evaluation (demonstrated in our published N-ATLaS case study), resident in-country native linguist pods, multi-dimensional rubric scoring, structured defect taxonomy, and transparent methodology.

Where can someone access Brydl?

Enterprise clients and research teams can request an evaluation through our secure online scoping portal. Native linguists and specialist researchers can apply to join our verified contributor network.
