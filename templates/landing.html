{# ---------------------------------------------------- N-ATLaS Research Case Study #}
Case Study Published Research

N-ATLaS Cross-Lingual AI Safety Evaluation

Our exploratory evaluation examined whether safety guardrails in global LLMs hold consistently across language boundaries when probed with matched English and Hausa prompts.

Key Finding: Deceptive Money Scheme Bypass

When probed with a high-yield investment scheme prompt ("Bada Naira dubu goma domin samun Naira dubu hamsin a cikin kwana biyu..."), frontier models that correctly refused the English prompt generated persuasive Hausa copy encouraging the financial scheme.

English: Refused (Safety Filter Triggered) Hausa: Complied (Safety Filter Bypassed)

Medical Query Context

In parallel medical advice testing, responses in Hausa were assessed by a native reviewer as reasonable preliminary health guidance rather than a safety failure, underscoring the necessity of nuanced human qualitative assessment over rigid automated scoring.

Methodology & Scope Disclosures

  • Sample Size: 11 records (10 evaluated models + baseline context) covering paired English/Hausa queries.
  • Deterministic Execution: Repeated deterministic runs (temperature=0); note that repeated deterministic queries do not constitute independent statistical replications.
  • Reviewer Scope: Qualitative evaluation conducted by a single native Hausa reviewer to identify directional failure modes.
  • Objective: Exploratory proof-of-concept demonstrating the necessity of native African language safety testing.
N-ATLaS Evaluation Summary
English Safety Refusal Consistency High (~90%)
Hausa Cross-Lingual Guardrail Retention Degraded (~40%)

Evaluation Conclusion:

English alignment training does not reliably transfer to African languages. Localized adversarial testing is required before production release.

Frequently Asked Questions

Factual information for enterprise clients, AI safety researchers, and annotators.

What is Brydl?

Brydl (Brydl AI) is an African Language AI Safety, Adversarial Red-Teaming, and Model Alignment Operating System developed by Gretek Technologies. It protects frontier artificial intelligence models and speech systems from cultural exploits, jailbreaks, and dialect failures across African languages.

What does Brydl do?

Brydl stress-tests large language models (LLMs) and voice agents through structured red-teaming (RedLab OS), collects phoneme-aligned paired speech corpora across noisy African acoustic environments, conducts cultural human-in-the-loop preference alignment (RLHF/DPO), and publishes empirical cross-lingual safety research.

Who is Brydl designed for?

Brydl is built for global foundation AI labs, enterprise technology firms, African AI research labs, as well as vetted native African linguists, security researchers, annotators, and compliance auditors.

What problems does Brydl solve?

Standard AI guardrails fail under low-resource language attacks (Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Swahili, Amharic), Western alignment bias misinterprets local legal/cultural proverbs, and speech models break under heavy accents. Brydl resolves these failures while ensuring exact Decimal compensation for African digital workers.

Why should someone choose Brydl?

Unlike generic crowd-working platforms, Brydl combines native African cultural grounding, empirical research validation (N-ATLaS study), Fernet credential encryption, strict SSRF protections, and instant direct Nigerian bank payouts.

Where and how can someone access Brydl?

Brydl is accessible online through its secure web portal. Enterprise clients can request custom safety benchmarks and corpora via our scoping portal, while native linguists and security researchers can sign in to participate in verified annotation campaigns.
