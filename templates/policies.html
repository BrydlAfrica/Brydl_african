Operational Standards

BRYDL CONTRIBUTOR POLICIES

These policies supplement the Contributor Terms & Conditions and govern all data evaluation, annotation, and red-teaming activities across the Platform.

Policy 1

Contributor Code of Conduct

Contributors must:

  1. Treat other contributors, reviewers, clients and Platform staff respectfully.
  2. Submit honest, original and good-faith work.
  3. Follow project instructions and disclose relevant limitations.
  4. Respect language, cultural and contextual differences.
  5. Avoid harassment, threats, discrimination and intimidation.
  6. Protect confidential information and personal data.
  7. Avoid conflicts of interest or disclose them promptly.
  8. Report suspected misconduct, fraud or security issues.
  9. Avoid misrepresenting qualifications, identity or experience.
  10. Use the Platform only for authorised activities.

Policy 2

Work Quality and Review Policy

  1. Every project must provide clear task instructions and acceptance criteria before work begins.
  2. Contributors must complete work independently unless collaboration is expressly permitted.
  3. Reviewers should assess submissions against stated criteria rather than personal preference.
  4. A rejection should include a reason or relevant quality category where reasonably practicable.
  5. Contributors should have a reasonable way to request clarification or appeal.
  6. Reviewers must not falsify scores, retaliate against contributors or manipulate acceptance decisions.
  7. Disagreement about language interpretation should be recorded where relevant, not automatically treated as poor performance.
  8. Project owners should monitor reviewer consistency and escalate recurring disagreements.
  9. Any gold-standard or reference answer must be protected from unauthorised disclosure.
  10. Quality thresholds and material changes must be communicated before they affect contributor compensation.
{# ---- Policy 3 ---- #}
Policy 3

Compensation and Payment Policy

  1. The compensation basis and rate must be disclosed before task acceptance.
  2. Contributors must be informed of relevant payment conditions, schedules, thresholds and transaction charges.
  3. Approved work must be recorded accurately.
  4. Payment status should distinguish at least: pending review, approved, payment processing, paid, disputed and rejected.
  5. A disputed task should not automatically place all unrelated approved earnings on hold.
  6. Payment holds must have a documented reason and be limited to what is reasonably necessary.
  7. Contributors must have a support route for payment questions (inquiry@brydldata.com.ng).
  8. The Platform must not advertise payment methods or timelines that have not been verified in production.
  9. Contributors must never be asked for a banking PIN, password or OTP.
  10. No guaranteed task volume or earnings should be implied.
{# ---- Policy 4 ---- #}
Policy 4

Confidentiality and Data Handling Policy

  1. Project information must be used only for the assigned purpose.
  2. Contributors must not publish or share task content, prompts, model responses, datasets or client materials without permission.
  3. Personal data must not be copied into unauthorised tools or external services.
  4. Contributors must use secure devices and protect account credentials.
  5. Contributors must not attempt to identify anonymised individuals or re-identify datasets.
  6. Suspected data exposure must be reported promptly.
  7. Project files must be deleted or returned when instructed, subject to lawful retention requirements.
  8. Access must be limited to people authorised for the relevant project.
  9. Administrators and reviewers must follow confidentiality obligations.
  10. Any project involving sensitive or special-category data requires additional documented safeguards.
{# ---- Policy 5 ---- #}
Policy 5

Sensitive Content and Wellbeing Policy

  1. Projects involving potentially disturbing material should disclose the nature of the content before acceptance where reasonably practicable.
  2. Contributors may decline a project before beginning it.
  3. Contributors should follow project-specific exposure limits and handling instructions.
  4. Contributors may report content that falls outside the stated project scope.
  5. Reviewers must not ridicule or penalise a contributor merely for raising a good-faith wellbeing concern.
  6. Any pause, reassignment or support process must be described accurately and offered only where operationally available.
  7. The Platform must not promise counselling or clinical support unless such a service has actually been arranged.
{# ---- Policy 6 ---- #}
Policy 6

Complaints and Appeals Policy

Contributors may raise complaints regarding task rejections, compensation, account restrictions, harassment, privacy, or working conditions. The complaint process should:

  1. Provide a clear contact channel: inquiry@brydldata.com.ng.
  2. Record the date, category and relevant project reference.
  3. Acknowledge receipt within a reasonable operational timeframe.
  4. Assign the issue to an appropriate reviewer who is not directly responsible for the disputed decision where practicable.
  5. Review relevant evidence and give a reasoned outcome.
  6. Explain any available escalation route.
  7. Protect complainants from retaliation for raising good-faith concerns.
  8. Keep complaint records only for as long as necessary and lawfully permitted.
{# ---- Policy 7 ---- #}
Policy 7

Account Security and Acceptable Use Policy

Contributors must not:

  • Share account credentials or impersonate another person.
  • Attempt to bypass access controls or exploit software vulnerabilities.
  • Upload malware, malicious files, or scrape data without authorisation.
  • Manipulate task assignment, qualification, or payment processes.
  • Use another contributor's account or submit work on their behalf without permission.
{# ---- Policy 8 ---- #}
Policy 8

Policy Administration

The Platform displays current policy versions, retains historical versions, trains authorised personnel, applies policies consistently, and avoids representing operational services or technical controls that have not been implemented.
