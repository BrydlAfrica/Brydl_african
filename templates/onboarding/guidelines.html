1. Objective Multi-Axis Ranking & Anti-Bias Standards

Evaluating pairwise model responses (Response A vs Response B) requires rigorous adherence to multi-dimensional rubrics:

  • Instruction Following: Did the model follow constraints (length, language, tone, negative constraints)?
  • Truthfulness & Grounding: Are all claims factual and free of hallucination?
  • Safety & Harm: Does either response offer actionable assistance on harmful tasks?
  • Helpfulness & Clarity: Is the tone natural, concise, and helpful?

1. Acoustic Fidelity & Verbatim African Diarization

Audio transcription and speech evaluation require strict adherence to acoustic capture standards:

  • Verbatim Accuracy: Transcribe exactly what is spoken, including false starts, filler words, and repetitions.
  • Acoustic Event Tags: Tag non-speech events using standardized tokens: [laughter], [applause], [inaudible], [crosstalk].
  • African Orthography: Ensure hooked letters (ɓ, ɗ, ƙ, ƴ) and tonal accents are accurately transcribed.

1. Exact Span Boundaries & Taxonomy Consistency

Named Entity Recognition (NER), intent tagging, and classification require pixel-perfect precision:

  • Exact Span Boundaries: Never include leading/trailing whitespace or extraneous punctuation in entity spans.
  • Taxonomy Adherence: Apply entity classes (Person, Location, Organization, DateTime) consistently according to guideline definitions.

1. Standard African Orthography & Hooked Consonants

Every submission in African languages (Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, etc.) must utilize the official orthography. For Hausa, standard hooked letters (ɓ, ɗ, ƙ, ƴ, Ɓ, Ɗ, Ƙ, Ƴ) are mandatory. Substituting standard letters (e.g. 'b' for 'ɓ' or 'd' for 'ɗ') constitutes a critical error.

Brydl's annotation interface provides an on-screen special character toolbar to insert all required characters effortlessly.
